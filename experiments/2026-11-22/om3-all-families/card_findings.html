At a glance: the Transformer ({comb:tf}; spread {sp:tf}) and the Transformer-Mamba hybrid ({comb:hyb}; {sp:hyb}) are level at the top: their spreads overlap, and the timing on this shared machine cannot separate them (with the second timing pass alone the hybrid leads, {ac:hyb:pass2} against 100.0). Mamba-2 is third ({comb:m2}; {sp:m2}) and the only model in the top four under all {nways} ways of counting (ranks {rr:m2}). Our v2 with positive decay ({comb:om2pos}) and RetNet ({comb:retnet}) follow. No model is best on every metric: on held-out text, which this page ranks first, the Transformer is {qro:tf} of 16 ({bpb:tf} bits/byte) and RetNet {qro:retnet} ({bpb:retnet}); both rank high on fast training steps. Best quality: our Mamba v1 ({bpb:om1}) and v2 with the always-on store ({bpb:om2a}), level ({ci:om1:om2a}). Fastest decoding: our Mamba v1 ({dec:om1} ms/token), which ranks {ro:om1} of 16 under these weights because each training step takes {xs:tf:om1} as long as the Transformer's and needs {xm:om1:tf} its training memory.   • Who leads depends on how costs are counted. With equal weights, {leadc:equal}; with quality at 50%, {leadc:q50}; with speed-heavy weights, {leadc:speed}; without the repeat-span column, {leadc:norec}; when training cost is counted as time to reach a given quality, {leadc:ttq}; when only inference counts, {leadc:infonly}; with memory at batch 8, {leadc:b8}. ("Level" means their spreads overlap.) Mamba-2 is in the top four under all {nways} ways, the hybrid under {top4:hyb} ({aro:hyb:stateonly} with OM2's state-only memory) and the Transformer under {top4:tf}: it is {aro:tf:ttq} with time to quality, {aro:tf:infonly} with inference only, {aro:tf:b8} with memory at batch 8 and {aro:tf:stateonly} with state-only memory.
  • Attention kernel. The attention layers (Transformer, hybrid, Griffin, Titans-style, the exact store) run on PyTorch's fused CPU attention kernel; the recurrent mixers run as ordinary PyTorch ops. With its attention written out in ordinary ops (the same function to {tfeq}), the Transformer takes {ctl:train}× as long per training step, needs {ctl:train_memory}× the training memory and prefills {ctl:prefill}× slower; its decode difference is not resolved (its rounds {ctl:dpl} ms against the Transformer's {ctl:dtf} ms in the same pass). It would score {ctl:comb}, inside the spreads of the rows ranked {ctl:near}. Part of the Transformer's lead is the kernel PyTorch ships, not the design.
  • Quality ranks differently. Our Mamba v1 ({bpb:om1}) and v2 with the always-on store ({bpb:om2a}) lead, level with each other. xLSTM's mLSTM ({bpb:mlstm}) is level with plain v2 ({bpb:om2}; {ci:mlstm:om2}, the two seeds {ps:mlstm:om2}) and ahead of Mamba-2 ({ci:mlstm:m2}). Every model except RetNet beats the Transformer ({bpb:tf}); RetNet is {ci:retnet:tf} behind. The RetNet paper's perplexity scaling comparison with Transformers is at 1.3B, 2.7B and 6.7B parameters, where it calls them comparable and reports RetNet ahead above about 2B, so it says nothing direct about 0.47M parameters; this arm's heads, value width and FFN are also narrower than the paper's.
  • Hybrids and recurrent designs. The hybrid (Mamba-2, attention + MLP, Mamba-2, attention + MLP: a 1:1 interleave where Jamba puts one attention layer in eight) is {ci:hyb:m2} behind the Mamba-2 arm and trains {xs:hyb:m2} faster per step; the gap is not the swap alone, since the Mamba-2 arm keeps Hugging Face's initialisation and the hybrid the benchmark's. Its two attention layers keep a KV cache ({state:hyb} KiB of decode state against Mamba-2's {state:m2}). Gated DeltaNet (mixer only) trails Mamba-2 ({ci:gdn:m2}); its paper reports it ahead at 1.3B parameters (Wiki perplexity 16.42 against 16.56), and we did not test why it is behind here: both arms are mixer-only with 32-wide key heads, narrower than the 128 the Gated DeltaNet paper chose, and the Gated DeltaNet arm has half as many heads and a 25% smaller recurrent state (6,144 against 8,192 values per layer). Griffin and the RWKV-7-style model are not separated (the two seeds {ps:griffin:rwkv7}) and both trail Mamba-2 (Griffin {ci:griffin:m2}). The Titans-style arm is Titans' MAG design (a 64-token attention window beside the long-term memory) with a small linear memory (2 heads of 32×32) instead of the deep MLP memory, a combination the paper does not report (its linear-memory ablation is of the memory-only model). With linear memory its update is the same kind of gated delta rule as Gated DeltaNet's, with a third of the memory state (2,048 against 6,144 values per layer), so its quality ({bpb:titans}) says nothing about Titans against Gated DeltaNet; it decodes slowest ({dec:titans} ms/token). Hyena edges the Transformer ({ci:hyena:tf}).
  • Speed and memory. Per training step the Transformer ({sps:tf} s) and RetNet ({sps:retnet}) are fastest, then Hyena ({sps:hyena}) and the hybrid ({sps:hyb}); our Mamba v1 is slowest ({sps:om1}). Counted as time to quality the order changes: the hybrid reaches the common dev-set target ({target} bits/byte, the worst final dev score of any single run; dev scores run lower than the test scores in the table) in {ttt:hyb} training minutes on this CPU and Mamba-2 in {ttt:m2}, against the Transformer's {ttt:tf}; the Transformer's own final dev score ({tffinal}) takes the hybrid {tttf:hyb} minutes and Mamba-2 {tttf:m2}, against the Transformer's {tttf:tf}. These are read off the dev curves of one 2,000-step cosine schedule; separately scheduled shorter runs were not trained. Griffin has the smallest decode state ({state:griffin} KiB, fixed in size because its attention is local). Hyena's per-sequence state, the history of its long-convolution inputs, is {state:hyena} KiB after 256 tokens, about the Transformer's {state:tf} KiB KV cache. The shared machine switched between a fast and a slow mode within tens of seconds: between timing rounds a model's decode time varied by up to {maxjit}% and its training step by up to {trjit}%, which is what the spreads show.
  • The "Mamba-4" store. The exact-token store helps quality when every token reads it (always-on minus placebo {ci:om2a:om2p}). The state-gated version does not work as designed: by the end of training its gate had not become selective (mean gate weight {gate:om2g} in the two seeds), and reading only where the gate exceeds 0.5, as designed, it reads on {read:om2g}% of tokens and scores {bpb:om2g} bits/byte, worse than plain v2 ({bpb:om2}). Used as a soft weight on every token it scores {bpbs:om2g} (soft minus placebo {ci:om2gsoft:om2p}). So it fails this page's test for state-gated retrieval (better exact recall without worse held-out loss) on held-out loss alone; this benchmark has no working recall measure, since no model copies.
  • No model shows in-context copying. Every model predicts a 32-byte span repeated after 16–176 bytes of another document worse than its first occurrence, which follows its own text (copy gain {cg} bits/byte over every model, seed and gap), so any copying is smaller than the cost of the change of context. The repeat-span column ({recrange}% next-byte accuracy) therefore measures ordinary next-byte skill, a second quality score, not recall; leaving it out changes no model's rank by more than {maxmove:norec}.
  • Choices made after seeing the results, and their effect. Memory: OM2's card (8 models) scored it by the decode state alone, which put Mamba-2 first and the Transformer 7th of 8; on these 16 models that definition puts {lead:stateonly} first, RetNet {aro:retnet:stateonly}, the hybrid {aro:hyb:stateonly} and the Transformer {aro:tf:stateonly}, because its 10% column multiplies Griffin's score by {spct:griffin}× while the 35% quality column moves no model by more than {qpct}% (the training-speed column, which multiplies our v1's score by {mult:om1:train_speed}×, was kept as a real difference in training time). Memory now counts fp32 weights plus the per-sequence decode state, which leaves out {tabs:hyena} KiB of input-independent filter taps that Hyena's decoder caches (counting them, Hyena is {aro:hyena:statetotal} instead of {ro:hyena}). Training memory is measured above the process baseline (with the whole process, as in OM2, {leadc:rsswhole}). Timings are medians over two passes, a choice made after both were seen: with the first pass alone {leadc:pass1}; with the second alone {leadc:pass2}; with each model's best decode and prefill round {leadc:latbest}; with prefill and decode timed interleaved in one process {leadc:latil}. Each of these is one of the {nways} ways of counting. The state-gated store is scored with its gate as designed; scored with its soft read it would be {wi:om2g_soft_rank} ({wi:om2g_soft_comb}) instead of {ro:om2g}. The recall column was renamed. Because these choices came after the results, and the top of this table matches the family ranking above (Transformer, hybrid, Mamba), that agreement is not independent support for that ranking.
  • Short context only. Every speed and memory figure is at 256 tokens or fewer, where the Transformer's KV cache ({state:tf} KiB) is still smaller than its weights; long contexts, where attention caches and decode time grow and fixed recurrent states do not, are not tested here.
