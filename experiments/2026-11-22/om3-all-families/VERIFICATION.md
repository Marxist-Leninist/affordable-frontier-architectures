# Verification of the OM3 card and findings

Three rounds of independent review, newest first after the summary: third round, second round, first round.

Before publication, the draft site card and the draft README findings (first version, generated from the first speed pass) were given to five independent reviewers, each with one lens and read-only access to the raw per-run results, the speed table, the code and the live page. Each was told to refute, not to confirm. They returned 74 findings (12 errors, 28 misleading, 34 minor). This file lists them all, and first what changed because of them.

## What changed after the first round

**Measurements (a second speed pass, `results/speed_table.json`; the first pass is kept as `results/speed_table_first_pass.json`).**

- Decode state is now the per-sequence part of the decode cache (batch-2 cache minus batch-1 cache). Hyena's decoder caches 1024 KiB of filter taps that depend only on the weights; the first pass counted them as state (2060 KiB instead of 1036).
- Training memory is the training step's peak above the process baseline (PyTorch import and data, about 0.46 GiB), not the whole process peak.
- The state-gated store is scored as designed (reads only where its gate exceeds 0.5) and its decode is timed with its trained gate. The first draft paired its soft-read quality (every token reads the store) with a decode timed on an untrained gate that opened on about half the tokens.
- A control was added: the Transformer with its attention written out in ordinary ops instead of PyTorch's fused CPU kernel. The first draft wrongly said no model used fused kernels; the attention layers (Transformer, hybrid, Griffin, Titans-style, the exact store) do.
- The decode index uses the median ms/token, as the bracket shows (it had used 1/mean).
- Prefill and decode are now each model's best per-process round over the two passes (same code path; the state-gated store
  only from the second pass, whose decode uses its trained gate). In the second pass other jobs on the shared machine slowed some
  short decode measurements by up to 30% while training steps stayed within a few per cent, so a median of rounds mixed
  interference into the 15% decode column. An interleaved timing of all seventeen models in one process (`latency_interleaved.py`,
  20 iterations in rotated order) was also run; in that setting the attention models decode relatively slower (the Transformer
  2.89 ms against 2.25 alone), so it is reported as one more way of counting rather than used for the headline. `merge_speed.py`
  builds the final table from the three measurements.
- The common dev target is the worst final score of any single run, so every model reaches it (RetNet did not, before).
- The seed x speed-round spread skips failed rounds instead of pairing different rounds.

**Index.** Four more ways of counting were added to the rank ranges (training cost as time to quality, bf16 weights, memory at batch 8, and prefill/decode timed interleaved in one process), the leader under each is reported, and the spread is shown under every combined score.

**Text.** Every number in the card and the README is filled from the data by `render_findings.py`. Corrected or qualified: "the Transformer leads whenever training cost counts" (false: in the first draft it was 4th with quality at 50% and 11th with state-only memory); "quality and inference favour Mamba-2" (quality at 50% puts the hybrid first); "steadiest" (replaced by the models in the top four under every way of counting); "best quality: our v1" (level with v2 + always-on store); the change of memory definition (now states that the OM2 card ranked Mamba-2 first and the Transformer 7th, and that the change came after the results); the "exact recall" column (renamed repeat-span accuracy: no model copies, so it measures next-byte skill); the reasons given for Gated DeltaNet trailing Mamba-2 (dropped: untested), for RetNet (its paper compares only at 1.3B-6.7B) and for Titans (its paper also claims gains at 4K context, with deep memory); the Titans fix status (its major finding was not fixed); the hybrid comparison (it also differs from the Mamba-2 arm in initialisation and MLPs); quality as the mean of three domains, not a pooled score; parameter counts (0.47-0.50M); the two training machines and PyTorch versions; intervals that leave out seed variation (per-seed values now given where the seeds disagree); the quality gate (ranks every model); the short-context limit; the reasons for leaving out diffusion models and Perceiver AR; and the links, which are now required before the card can be built.

**Not changed, with reasons.** The stated weights (kept from OM2 for continuity; alternatives are reported). The training-speed column (its wide range reflects real differences in training time). Titans' deep memory (not implemented; the arm is labelled as the paper's linear-memory ablation). Hyena's float64 FFT and the Titans-style exact inner step (disclosed as slower than the papers' recipes).

## Third round

The third draft (timings paired with the Transformer timed in the same round, "=" tie groups by paired wins) was given to two more reviewers, asked for errors and misleading statements only. They returned 18 findings (6 errors, 12 misleading). Every number in the drafts matched the data except the control's score.

**What changed after the third round (final version, not reviewed again).**

- Pairing with the Transformer's same round was dropped: the machine switched between a fast and a slow mode within tens of seconds, so a model and the Transformer timed minutes apart in one round often caught different modes and pairing added the Transformer's own variation. Every timing is now the plain median over the rounds of both passes. The first pass alone, the second pass alone, the best rounds and the interleaved timing are alternative ways of counting, so their effect on the top two is shown (either may lead).
- The spread now pairs each round's own timings with the Transformer's medians (so the Transformer has a spread too), and rows are marked "≈" only against the row above (no chained tie groups). Leaders under the alternatives are called level by the same overlap rule.
- Corrected: the first draft used median timings (not best rounds); the README's byte-stream wording (same stream for every arm at each seed); the control's rank is given as the spreads it falls inside, and its decode difference as not resolved; the Transformer's ranks outside the top three are all listed; the soft-read what-if for the gated store is given; the closing note says only the eight new arms were written from their papers.
- Not changed: the live page's other sections that call the state-gated retrieval idea untested (they belong to other lanes; the suggested wording was posted to them).

## Second round

The revised draft (second speed pass, best-round timings, interleaved timing as an alternative, rewritten index) was given to three more independent reviewers (numbers and code, claims, reader). They returned 49 findings (6 errors, 15 misleading, 28 minor). The full list is below.

**What changed after the second round.**

- The timing choices made after seeing results were not neutral: with the Transformer first only under the exact combination used (best rounds over both passes plus training memory above the baseline), and the hybrid ahead under most others, every timing index is now the median over all rounds of both passes of the speed relative to the Transformer timed in the same round (paired, so slow-downs that hit the whole machine cancel).
