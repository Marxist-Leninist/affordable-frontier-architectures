How firm is it

  • Other weightings. Across 20,000 random weightings of the available lines, DLGM #458 comes out ahead in 14% and Fusion #879 in 86%.
  • Dropping one line. The same name stays on top whichever single line of evidence is removed.
  • Seed noise on your objective. At equal steps Fusion minus DLGM is +0.024 nats with a standard error of 0.008 (3.1 standard errors). That is a real difference at this sample size.
  • Your matched small run is a statistical tie: the per-seed Fusion minus DLGM differences are -0.0052, +0.0026, +0.0296 on WikiText and -0.0016, -0.0017, +0.0665 on FineWeb, so the average lead of DLGM comes from one seed.
  • The paper numbers are single runs with no error bars. At 1.3B parameters only Fusion was trained upstream: 52.01 on 14 accuracy metrics against 51.04 for DeltaNet.
  • Serving readiness is a rubric, not a measurement. Exact incremental cache verified against full recompute on three trained checkpoints (worst error 1.3e-14 in float64, 1.0e-5 in float32); fixed state of 103,680 bytes; public at experiments/asi_arch_psfr_cache_v1. Incremental cache attempts v63 and v64 reproduced the same tokens (RMS error about 1e-6) but failed the strict exact-match gate twice; the Transformer baseline failed the same gate.

Known limits of tonight’s Fusion run

  • Retention did nothing in this run. Fusion’s per-head forgetting factor starts at 0.99996 in the original and the lowest value any head reached was 0.99996, so its memory behaved like DLGM’s plain delta rule. What separated the two here is the gating and the FIR filters. Mean gate at the end (short FIR, long FIR, delta memory, value): 0.25, 0.50, 0.72, 0.61.
  • One shared port convention is not neutral. Both ports use the block’s own whole-width output norm instead of the per-head norm of the originals. Fusion’s four independent gates can then also change how loud a head is; DLGM’s gates always sum to one. Part of any Fusion advantage could come from that.
  • Speed columns are not like for like. The DLGM port computes its memory in chunks of 16 tokens, Fusion needs 32 (its forgetting is tied to the chunk). Same mathematics for DLGM, different step time.
  • Three seeds, a 12-layer mirror, 1,200 steps. This is a ranking signal under your objective, not a full-scale training result.

Both candidates keep every binary matrix of the live block (886.9 billion latents at 560 blocks), so storage and streaming are the same for both. The faithful forms add about 58.5 and 60.4 billion 32-bit parameters for their routers and gates, which is too much to train at this size; the low-rank forms add 10.1 and 17.8 million, under a tenth of a gigabyte, which is nothing. So the low-rank form is the one that could run, and on size the two are equal.

“Equal time” is the simulator’s model, not a full-scale measurement. In the small mirror a candidate step costs two to three times a live-block step, because at width 256 the mixer’s convolutions and memory updates are large next to tiny matrices. At width 8192 the arithmetic runs the other way: a block spends about 800 million multiply-adds per token in its binary matrices, attention over 2,048 tokens about 34 million, and either mixer about 6 million. By that count both candidates would cost about the same per step as the live block, slightly less, and the two differ from each other by well under 1%. That is an estimate from counting operations; nobody has timed either mixer inside the real trainer.

How the index is built

For a gain component each candidate scores 100 x max(gain, 0) / max(gain of DLGM, gain of Fusion, floor). The better one gets 100 when its gain clears the noise floor, the other gets its share of that gain, and a candidate that does not beat the shared baseline gets 0. Where a component has sub-experiments they are scored separately and averaged. Index = weighted sum of the component scores (0 to 100).

The name on top is the higher index. A margin under 3 points is called level in words.

What was run tonight

  • A new simulator plugin, fusion879.py, ports ASI-Arch #879 with the same layout and conventions as the existing DLGM port (dlgm458.py). The file name switches it to the low-rank form.
  • Fidelity check: with the original #879 weights copied in, the port reproduces the original’s output to 8.9e-16 in 64-bit, and to 1.3e-06 in 32-bit on the GPU with the original library’s kernels untouched, at six sequence lengths. No future-token leak. Test, result.
  • Three seeds of each form on the GPU queue, plus a same-night control run of the live block for timing. Raw runs: unified.json on the main ASI-Arch index.
  • A three-reader review of the plugin before it was queued found no blocking defect; its caveats are the limits listed above.
